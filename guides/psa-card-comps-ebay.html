How to find PSA 10 and graded card comps on eBay

A comp is only useful if it is the same card in the same grade. Most bad comps come from mixing grades, counting lots, or trusting a crossed-out Best Offer price.

1. Search the sold listings

  1. Sign in to eBay. Since July 2026 eBay only shows sold listings to signed-in users.
  2. Search the card the way sellers title it: year, set, player or Pokémon, card number, grader and grade. Example: 2023 Charizard ex 199/165 PSA 10.
  3. Open Filter and turn on Sold Items. eBay shows roughly the last 90 days.
  4. Sort by Ended recently so the newest sales come first. Card prices move fast; a sale from two months ago can be far off today.

2. Keep grades apart

A search for “PSA 10” still returns PSA 9s, BGS 9.5s and raw copies whose titles mention PSA. Read each title, or exclude the others with a minus sign: Charizard 199/165 PSA 10 -9 -BGS -CGC.

GradeWhat to compare against
PSA 10Other PSA 10s only. A PSA 10 often sells for several times a PSA 9.
BGS 9.5 / SGC 10 / CGC 10The same grader and grade. Different graders sell at different prices for the same number.
RawRaw copies in similar condition. Leave out anything slabbed.

3. Leave out what misleads

  • Best Offer sales. When a seller accepts an offer, eBay shows the original asking price crossed out. The real price was lower and is hidden, so skip those.
  • Lots and bundles. “Lot of 3” or “PSA 10 + PSA 9” prices are for several cards.
  • Outliers. A $1 shill sale or a mistaken $5,000 sale can drag an average. Use the median, not the average.

4. Check the trend

Compare the median of the last 7 days with the weeks before. If the last week is clearly higher, the card is heating up; if lower, wait before you buy.
